## Evidence and limits by investigation

Read each row alongside the baseline judgement above. These are the specific reasoning demands used for the audit. A baseline revision question establishes representation. The transfer column identifies a guided route delivered here or a remaining demand; it does not certify competence.

|RP and purpose|Apparatus, method and measured quantity|Control and reason for procedure|Measurement problem, interpretation and justified improvement|Independent transfer in catch-up or remaining demand|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|1 Resolve cells|Slide, objective, calibrated scale; measure image or cell length|Thin specimen permits light transmission|Mis-calibration biases lengths; recalibrate at the selected magnification|Choice of scale remains|
|2 Investigate osmosis|Weigh potato before and after immersion|Equal dimensions and duration; blot surface water|Attached water raises final reading; interpret percentage mass change|Existing osmosis and revision data|
|3 Identify food molecules|Use reagent-specific colour observation|Known positive and negative samples distinguish reagent failure|Ambiguous colour or cross-contamination; clean separate droppers|Evaluating an ambiguous negative remains|
|4 Estimate amylase activity|Buffered mixture, bath, iodine wells; record first starch-negative time|Same temperature and concentrations isolate pH|Interval sampling bounds the endpoint; shorter intervals improve resolution|Evaluate temperature confound|
|5 Investigate light and photosynthesis|Collect oxygen volume over known time|Same plant, carbon dioxide supply and temperature|Bubble sizes vary; measure volume per time|Existing light/temperature model|
|6 Compare responses|Align zero, release ruler and record catch distance|Same hand and release conditions; vary delay to reduce anticipation|Practice can mimic treatment; counterbalance trial order|Evaluate quiet-first trials|
|7 Investigate abundance or distribution|Count quadrats of known area at random coordinates or along transects|Equal quadrat area makes densities comparable|Edge-only counts bias meadow estimate; sample across field or replicate transects|Evaluate stream-bank claim|
|8 Make soluble salt|Warm acid, add excess insoluble solid, filter, crystallise|Excess solid consumes acid; remove unreacted solid before concentration|Wet crystals inflate mass; dry before weighing|Choice of purification steps remains|
|9 Identify electrode products|Inert electrodes and dc supply; observe deposit and gas tests|Keep electrode material fixed; separate products|Bubbles alone cannot identify gas; test a separate sample|Transfer from molten salt to solution|
|10 Compare energy changes|Probe in cup; record starting and extreme temperatures|Equal amounts and starting temperatures isolate comparison|Heat exchange reduces measured rise; insulate and monitor peak|Evaluate a delayed measurement|
|11 Compare reaction rates|Measure gas volume against time or time a visible endpoint|Keep quantities, temperature and apparatus comparable|Gas leaks reduce readings; check seal. Gradient represents rate|Choosing between rate methods remains|
|12 Identify mixture components|Paper, solvent, pencil baseline; measure spot and solvent distances|Same solvent for comparison; baseline above liquid|Overlapping spots complicate identification; compare standards or another solvent|Existing chromatography sequence|
|13 Assess and purify water|Evaporate measured sample, weigh residue; distil another sample|Use equal volumes to compare dissolved solids|Incomplete drying increases residue mass; reheat to constant mass|Evaluating distillate remains|
|14 Estimate specific heat capacity|Block, heater, thermometer; measure mass, input energy and temperature rise|Insulate to reduce energy transfer to surroundings|Temperature rise too small for measured input biases calculated capacity high|Justifying an energy-loss correction remains|
|15 Investigate resistance|Measure voltage and current for wire lengths or combinations|Same wire material and diameter; limit heating|Temperature changes resistance; switch off between readings|Temperature controls remain|
|16 Characterise components|Ammeter in series, voltmeter across component; vary potential difference|Keep resistor temperature steady for ohmic comparison|Lamp curve shows changing resistance; repeat both polarities|Existing I–V model|
|17 Determine density|Balance plus dimensions or displaced volume|Fully submerge irregular object; remove bubbles|Bubbles inflate apparent volume; repeat after dislodging them|Choice of apparatus remains|
|18 Test proportionality|Read unloaded and loaded lengths with fixed ruler; subtract|Same spring and reference point; wait for oscillations|Parallax shifts reading; use pointer at eye level. Unload to test recovery|Interpret departure from straight line|
|19 Investigate acceleration|Trolley, hanger and gates; measure acceleration|Transfer masses to vary force at fixed moving mass|Friction changes resultant force; keep track level and friction comparable|Evaluate adding masses to hanger|
|20 Measure waves|Calibrated image and timer; measure crest intervals and oscillations|Use a fixed position to measure frequency|Counting crests instead of intervals biases wavelength; measure multiple intervals|Distinguish rope motion from pattern speed|
|21 Compare infrared transfer|Cube and detector for emission; matched plates and lamp for absorption|Same initial temperature, area, distance and orientation|A changing cube temperature affects signal; monitor and repeat comparisons|Evaluate tray versus block|
