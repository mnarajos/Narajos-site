# Site-curated science vocabulary review

This reference is curated for Narajos and aligned to the relevant curricula. It is not an official exam-board glossary. The stored entries keep an identifier, meaning, variants and searchable synonyms, course, subject, topic or unit, tier, learning status, display policy, source and, where useful, a catch-up destination. Course status and the decision to highlight a particular occurrence are separate. Definitions remain available in the topic's word-help lookup even when ordinary reading does not highlight them.

The counts above describe reference entries, not the number of highlighted words. One entry may apply to several topics. Candidate scans assist editorial review but do not establish that a lesson’s important vocabulary is covered. The current reference is a starting selection, and a passage-by-passage review remains needed across the courses. AAQ cross-unit prerequisites were checked against current F170–F177 teaching text; ambiguous senses retain contextual matching, but this does not establish complete unit coverage.

The review compared the site's wording with its course and unit sequence and the specifications, retained the existing explicit KS3 annotations, and checked subject senses, examples and tier status. It uses original student-facing wording rather than copying a list. For example, the osmosis and diffusion senses were checked against [OpenStax's account of passive transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-2-passive-transport); instrument resolution was checked against [AQA's GCSE science subject-specific vocabulary](https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/resources/science/AQA-SCIENCE-GCSE-SUBJECT-VOCAB.PDF); and the transplant example was checked against [OpenStax's pharmacology discussion](https://openstax.org/books/pharmacology/pages/6-3-immunosuppressants-biologics-monoclonal-antibodies-and-biosimilar-drugs). The actual source URLs are kept with each entry.

## Tailoring and interaction

- **KS3 to GCSE:** KS3 calls a cell “a tiny living building block of an organism” and uses a cheek cell as an example. GCSE calls it “the smallest unit of a living organism” and relates its structures to life processes.
- **Shared tier meaning:** Foundation and Higher both see the same definition of diffusion. A tier controls curriculum coverage; it does not control access to more word help.
- **Different senses:** the biology nucleus contains genetic material; the atomic nucleus is the central part of an atom containing protons and neutrons. Microscopy resolution and instrument resolution are separate entries.
- **Applied AAQ:** immunosuppression is a reduction in immune activity; a general transplant-medicine example explains both reduced rejection risk and increased vulnerability to infection.
- **Selective and increased support:** the same [Year 9 cell-structures passage](https://narajos.com/science-catch-up/lesson/?topic=cell-structures) offers more links in catch-up's default mode than with an explicit selective preference. [Increased support](site-vocabulary-review/gcse-catchup-more.png) · [Selective support](site-vocabulary-review/gcse-catchup-selective.png).
- **Assessment:** in the field-count question, looking up a *quadrat* leaves a correct calculation independent. In “What is diffusion?”, the definition supplies the tested knowledge; confirming the reveal records help, and closing the offer does not. Inline controls and lookup use the same rule. [Incidental help](site-vocabulary-review/gcse-incidental-help.png) · [Assessed knowledge](site-vocabulary-review/gcse-target-help.png).
- **Interface:** [KS3 desktop](site-vocabulary-review/ks3-desktop.png) · [KS3 phone](site-vocabulary-review/ks3-phone.png) · [AAQ desktop](site-vocabulary-review/human-biology-desktop.png). ## Assessment and remaining gaps

A question-specific allowlist identifies incidental terms and assessed targets only after the question, choices and complete definition have been reviewed together. A confirmed target reveal uses the activity's existing helped or hinted state. Unreviewed definitions are withheld during an active assessment, including from search, rather than guessing whether they reveal the answer. Reviewed examples currently include GCSE diffusion, ecology sampling, exothermic reactions and electrical measurement; the Year 10 vaccine and photosynthesis checks; and an AAQ F170 guided question. Completed feedback and non-assessment teaching are eligible for normal help.

This conservative rule means most individual GCSE, KS3 and AAQ question prompts do not yet offer incidental lookup during an active attempt. Years 10–11 begin in supported teaching mode; starting a check applies the reviewed assessment rules. If an unfinished check is paused, lookup stays restricted until it is resumed or completed so the same attempt cannot bypass those rules. Saved responses remain available on resume. Older records without the new attempt flag are treated as unfinished when they contain an open check, response or help state. Previous versions could open a check automatically, so this can conservatively restrict lookup even when the student had not interacted with that check. Further prompt-by-prompt editorial review is needed before expanding those allowlists. Static older KS3 checks do not record an independent mastery score. Standalone simulations are not yet integrated: several are explicitly A-level, while the enzyme-kinetics simulation serves more than one audience. Their interactive layouts, changing text and activity boundaries need their own integration review. These are specific rollout gaps, not claims of complete occurrence-level assessment review. Broader independent subject-specialist review and a hands-on screen-reader test are also still desirable.
