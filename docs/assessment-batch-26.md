## Biological Drawing

The source field, accepted drawing and two rejected drawings were rendered side by side for each of the six specimens. The checks follow the entire leader from the label to its tip. A line identifying an internal region must cross its enclosing boundary. The tests reject extra crossings and distinguish a boundary label from a label inside an open space.

Privet, arterial wall and cheek observations have no reliable source calibration. The judgement and its feedback explicitly limit a successful comparison to visible morphology and relative proportions. The adjacent onion/privet comparison illustrates when to draw a single cell and when to show only tissue boundaries. The paper task uses a separate neighbouring onion cell and asks the pupil to review the title, outline, line quality, ruled label and measured bar with units. Earlier checklist selections retain their meaning. Paper work stays self-reviewed.

The independent resizing question uses the photographed buttercup bar. At 0.31 mm per 123 image units, the 246-unit segment represents 620 µm. A 36 mm drawing of that segment needs a 5.8 mm line to represent 100 µm, to one decimal place. The task saves the pupil’s entry and first submission. A clue, a wrong answer followed by correction and a first-attempt success produce different feedback. The calculation has its own record and leaves the six-drawing score intact.
