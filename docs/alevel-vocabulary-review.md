# AQA A-level Biology vocabulary review

## Sources and editorial decisions

The Definitions resource for each of the eight PMT topics supplied the initial inventory. The review compared that inventory with AQA 7402 sections 3.1–3.8 and the language in Narajos explanations, worked steps, questions, practicals and evidence activities. The vocabulary files contain original pupil-facing definitions and link to the references used for each meaning. [PMT AQA topic index](https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/biology-revision/a-level-aqa/) · [AQA subject content](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/subject-content)

The review covered the concept notes for all 171 concepts in topics 1–8 and checked sampled question stems and feedback against their teaching context. App vocabulary beyond the PMT inventory includes *water potential*, *graticule*, *oncotic pressure*, *DCPIP*, *uncoupler* and *GLUT4*. The subject checks also distinguish reduced NAD from reduced NADP, production from productivity, and a carrier of a genetic variant from a membrane carrier. Topic 2 notes clarify that Golgi transport vesicles have different functions and that an antigen need not be a foreign protein. [OpenStax Biology 2e](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-introduction) · [AQA practical assessment](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/practical-assessment)

AQA's measurement vocabulary distinguishes accuracy from precision, instrument resolution from uncertainty, and repeatability from reproducibility. The practical and mathematical appendices locate these terms in the course. The statistical entries check the meaning of a P value and distinguish a statistically significant result from a biologically important effect. [AQA measurement vocabulary](https://www.aqa.org.uk/resources/science/as-and-a-level/teach/subject-specific-vocabulary) · [AQA practical assessment](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/practical-assessment) · [AQA mathematical requirements](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/mathematical-requirements-and-exemplifications) · [NIST statistics handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc131.htm) · [American Statistical Association statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-valuestatement.pdf)

The review followed the technical wording through each sentence. Terms such as *interstitial fluid*, *electrochemical gradient* and *percentage points* have precise jobs in the passage. Helpful aliases include *tissue fluid* and *red blood cell*. Academic words such as *infer*, *inhibit* and *proportional* can affect comprehension across topics. RP11 uses “remaining blue colour” and “fresh portion of the sample” in place of “residual blue colour” and “fresh aliquot”. It retains *absorbance*, *calibration range* and *dilution factor*.

## Vocabulary in assessments

- **Incidental help.** During Interpret data, the definition of *immerse* explains that the liquid covers the sample. A student's ability to calculate a percentage change is assessed separately. The student can open the definition inline or find it by searching for *immersion*. [Interpret data](https://narajos.com/alevel/#exam-evidence)
- **Help with assessed knowledge.** A Topic 1 question asks what hydrolysis means. Opening the term or selecting it in lookup prompts the student to confirm that the definition will count as help. If they choose to read it, the lesson's existing supported-answer handling applies. Closing the prompt before reading the explanation leaves the attempt independent. [Monomers and polymers questions](https://narajos.com/alevel/#lesson-a1-monopoly)
- **Other active questions.** Existing assessments use reviewed activity-specific help where available. Unreviewed definitions are withheld during an active attempt, including from search. Practical setup vocabulary has a separate reviewed list, and eligible explanations and feedback can offer definitions once the relevant answer is complete.

## Maintaining the reference

The topic files document curriculum status, prerequisites and common forms of each term. The skills file covers practical, measurement, mathematical and academic language across topics. An entry's `support` controls how often help appears. Its `status`, `topics` and `prerequisites` identify where it belongs in the course and what the explanation assumes.

1. Review the intended meaning in the current passage. Give distinct scientific senses distinct stable IDs and use context cues where a short form is ambiguous.
2. Keep spelling and grammatical variants in `variants`. Use `synonyms` for related lookup terms when substituting them into the original wording would change the sense.
3. Link the checked source and the corresponding catch-up concept, if one exists. Read the definition on its own to check that it uses easier language than the term being explained.
4. Review the meaning, clarification, example, synonyms and catch-up destination against the current assessment target. A definition that supplies assessed knowledge must use that activity's help convention. Check lookup and inline help with the same policy.
5. Check the passage in selective and increased modes. Rephrase unnecessary difficulty and use a full phrase where it carries the meaning. A deliberately reviewed core term can remain available from lookup while absent from the inline highlights.
