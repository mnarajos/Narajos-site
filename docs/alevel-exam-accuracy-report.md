# AQA A-level Biology accuracy and Exam Skills review

## Scientific accuracy and assessment

- **Gene expression.** The original question inferred a transcription rate from an mRNA amount and confused new protein synthesis with the amount of protein present. The replacement gives separate measurements for mRNA amount, transcription rate and the rate of new transporter synthesis. The feedback explains why reduced translation initiation fits the measurements, why reduced transcription conflicts with them, and why faster breakdown concerns protein abundance. It presents reduced initiation as a possible mechanism.
- **Practicals and data.** The revised activities distinguish raw absorbance, pigment concentration, pigment released and rate. They specify matched blanks, calibration and sample volumes where the inference depends on them. Other corrections qualify a respirometer temperature comparison, avoid biased field selection when counting mitotic cells, calculate comparable average uptake rates, and distinguish an observed river sample count from population abundance and a causal claim.
- **Mechanisms and statistics.** The review made the electrochemical conditions for sodium ion entry explicit, clarified net osmosis and ADH, and distinguished dissolved oxygen and partial pressure from oxygen carried by haemoglobin. It added conditions to inhibitor and Hardy–Weinberg questions and clarified what a p value, a test threshold, error bars and repeated measurements can establish. The sampled temperature with the highest mean is described as the highest observed value, with finer sampling needed to estimate the optimum.
- **Core topic wording.** Tissue-fluid explanations now identify the assumptions behind venous reabsorption in the classroom model and include lymphatic return. The insulin lesson distinguishes GLUT4 recruitment in muscle and adipose tissue from liver glycogen storage. Other changes clarify glycogen breakdown, murein and peptidoglycan, and the scope of the genetic code.

## Curriculum and contextual vocabulary

The assessment review used official AQA June 2022–2025 papers, mark schemes and examiner reports across Papers 1–3, with older papers for targeted terminology. It distinguishes specification content, credit in individual questions, language pupils may need to interpret and optional enrichment. For example, AQA specifies **gluconeogenesis** and has assessed it directly. **Endothelium** appears in a credited structure–function explanation. **Troponin** helps explain muscle contraction and its role is explicitly outside the specification requirement. The evidence report records the seven requested terminology decisions and the question-specific evidence for accepting **GLUT4** or **carrier protein**. [Assessment and terminology evidence](ALEVEL_AQA_ASSESSMENT_EVIDENCE.md)

An authored term can open a short in-place definition from a shared vocabulary registry. The kidney worked example introduces **interstitial fluid** using the familiar term **tissue fluid**. A-level topic introductions also use the registry for **endothelium**, **oncotic pressure** and **gluconeogenesis**. The definition opens with a tap, click or keyboard, and closes with Escape, a close button or an outside click or tap. Only deliberately marked teaching text carries definitions. Independent response screens omit vocabulary cues and hints. The checklist and model become available after the student submits a draft for self-review.

## Understand the question

Each sequence moves from a concise explanation to a worked example that students can think through before revealing the reasoning. A guided activity then rehearses the skill. Students write in a different context before they can see the model and checklist. Original drafts, revisions and self-review judgements stay in local browser storage. The previous activity’s record remains available, including the earlier written response where one exists. New progress is recorded by sequence.

| Sequence | Learning objective | Student action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify the target | Name the biological entity and outcome the question asks about. | Identify the failed event in mitosis, then explain a specific outcome of flooding in a different context. |
| Interpret command words | Distinguish describing a result from explaining it. | Identify a description that covers the full temperature range, then describe a new pattern using the data. |
| Select and use evidence | Choose data that support the claim under the stated conditions. | Select readings at the same light intensity, then make paired comparisons in a new table. |
| Respect the conditions | Check each proposed explanation against the measurements and assumptions. | Assess explanations for the transporter measurements, then distinguish substrate use and ATP production in a new context. |
| Explain and compare | Compare the same feature in both cases and link a mechanism to the measured outcome. | Follow the beetroot example through pigment release and light absorption, then write a new paired explanation. |
| Answer every demand | Identify each demand in a combined question and respond to each. | Match statements to a practical suggestion and its justification, then write both parts of a new response. |
| Apply the skills independently | Use appropriate evidence and state how far it supports a claim. | Evaluate a pond investigation and separate the measured result from the proposed mechanism. |

## Neighbouring activities

| Activity | Current instruction and changes | Remaining development |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain precisely | Progressively models an adequate explanation and finishes with written self-review. The sodium, osmosis and valve explanations now state the conditions and causal links explicitly. | Add a guided edit of a partial explanation, then use a fresh written context for each skill. |
| Reason through practicals | Models how a method affects a claim and ends with an independent method comparison. The revision improves the absorbance, sampling and respirometer tasks. | Ask students to choose a comparison or measurement and justify it before they see the available explanations. |
| Interpret data | Models cumulative measurements and average rate. The revision improves calibration, statistical inference and evaluation of field observations. | Give rate, calibration and statistical inference their own worked-to-guided-to-independent sequences so students practise the same reasoning on fresh evidence. |

Student improvement remains unmeasured. The most direct follow-up is to compare pupils’ written responses to unfamiliar questions before and after using the sequences, applying the same criteria for relevance, evidence and completeness.
