## Changes to assessment

- Plausible alternatives now test follicle development versus ovulation, airway and alveolar mechanisms, the purpose of a solvent control, antibody structure and memory, classification of positive screens, measurement bias and causal inference. F175 changes are limited to two existing general knowledge prompts on hormone roles and fertilisation outside the body.
- Feedback follows the scientific claim chosen by the learner, even when choices appear in a different order. Numerical feedback identifies specific denominator, conversion, rounding and operation errors when the answer matches a known error. Grouping and sequencing prompt focused correction and retain valid matches or classifications. Retargeted practice uses related items with different evidence or a different application.
- The inhibition-zone task now uses an authored table and asks for an inference justified by the measurements. The screening sequence uses a fresh contingency table. A separate monitoring dataset includes changed measurement conditions and missing data. A fictional randomised trial question distinguishes uncertainty in an estimate from confounding and limits on longer-term evidence. The tissue question reuses a licensed testis field with descriptive alt text and asks for a visible reason for interpretation. A genuinely fresh field remains a useful next assessment sample.
- The extended cases offer two plausible responses and a commentary on how the stronger response uses mechanism, context, evidence and qualification. Pupils can see their original response, revise it and finish a question-specific self-review. Review and objective accuracy stay separate. OCR's level-of-response format informed the examples without assigning an automatic score.
- F171 expanded from 16 to 42 spaced-review cards, distributed **6, 15, 10, 7 and 4** across its five topic areas. Existing card IDs retain their review history. Learners can type an answer or indicate that they recalled it mentally before revealing. A blank reveal leads to another retrieval attempt.
- Six mixed-method questions use the existing Skills Practice entry point and exam engine. Learners choose a comparison, apply a baseline percentage, take a rate from a cumulative heartbeat graph, rearrange a mean, handle a missing reading, and interpret repeatability against a checked reference. The mixed set has its own saved session and uses authored values. The existing 14 generated families retain their original structure.
- The fixed bank now contains 561 items including rotating variants. Three are new independent transfers and six form the mixed-method activity. The 49 Build Understanding sequences, Quick Mix and 44 guided routes continue to use the existing teaching and assessment flow.
- The [question-writing standard](HUMAN_BIOLOGY_QUESTION_WRITING_STANDARD.md) now documents explicit Check answer submission, option-specific diagnosis, authored numerical error feedback, written self-review, progress classifications and accessibility checks.

## Before and after

The previous inhibition-zone item asked the learner to choose the largest circle. The revised item gives measured zones of 14 mm and 20 mm and a carrier-only result with no clear zone. The learner must decide what the data justify. A larger measured zone supports a difference under the test conditions. Claims about diffusion, susceptibility category or clinical effectiveness require other evidence. Each incorrect option now tests one of those inferences. The task requires scientific interpretation as well as reading a value.

The previous ovulation variant contrasted an LH surge with ADH and insulin. It now contrasts LH with the earlier effects of FSH and oestrogen and the role of progesterone after ovulation. Each alternative corresponds to a plausible confusion about when the hormone acts.

Automated checks exercise shuffled choice feedback, explicit submission, keyboard focus, touch, saved written revisions, flashcard spacing and progress history, numerical marking, targeted reteaching and resumption. The browser suites cover narrow and desktop layouts, enlarged text, accessible chart tables, automated accessibility checks and the existing Catch-up, F175 reproduction and question journeys. Pupil trials, physical-device and specialist screen-reader sessions remain outstanding. The audit does not establish population-level discrimination or comprehensive specification coverage.
