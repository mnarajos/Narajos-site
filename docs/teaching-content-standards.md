# Teaching content on Narajos

Start with the essential idea a pupil needs to understand. Choose a prompt that makes that idea worth thinking about. Model the reasoning in short steps, then use a check that distinguishes a likely misunderstanding from a secure answer. Build in feedback, another attempt and a fresh application where useful.

## Scientific explanations

Teach the Single Paragraph Outline explicitly at KS3, GCSE, A-level and Human Biology. Adapt the scientific content and language to the learner's prior knowledge.

- The topic sentence answers the question or establishes the main scientific claim.
- The supporting notes in the outline organise relevant evidence, mechanisms or causes. In the draft, each note becomes a clear supporting sentence.
- The concluding sentence states an implication or judgement justified by that explanation. It can be omitted when a short answer has already fulfilled the task.
- Label these roles in introductory examples. Introduce because, but and so after the basic structure is clear. Use more sophisticated syntax and qualifications where they sharpen the science.
- Apply the structure to new extended model answers and feedback. In existing content, prioritise answers whose opening misses the point or whose steps obscure the scientific reasoning. Keep factual quiz feedback and concise exam responses proportionate.

## Visuals

A visual can invite curiosity and discussion, show a scientific idea, or do both. Include the first type when it provides a worthwhile observation, puzzle or conversation. Include the second when pupils need to see a structure, process or comparison. Omit purely decorative images that compete for attention.

- Show something a pupil can discuss with a teacher, TA or other adult. Use a brief optional prompt, such as ‘What changes between these two pictures?’
- In explanatory models, keep labels close to the relevant features. Show one meaningful change at a time. Provide text equivalents, paired with shapes or labels where colour also carries meaning.
- Use controllable motion, meaningful pause or step controls, and enough time to inspect a still. Check narrow-screen, keyboard and screen-reader use.
- For reused images, check that the licence permits reuse and any edits, then credit the title, creator, linked source, linked licence and the changes made.

## Checks and privacy

Use deterministic checking for answers the software can judge reliably. For open scientific prose, give question-specific criteria, a model and a chance to revise. Treat understandable scientific errors as genuine attempts and use them to prompt feedback and reflection. Keep pupil drafts and progress on the device. Avoid bespoke speech controls and semantic marking claims.
