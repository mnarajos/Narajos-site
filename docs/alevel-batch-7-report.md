# A-level Biology Batch 7

## Teaching and activities

The A-level companion now includes ten teaching families with 52 original tasks. Each family contains a stated scientific principle, a stepped worked example, guided practice, independent application, graduated hints, explanatory feedback, and a related catch-up route. The data file also records the misconception and specification mapping for future authoring.

- Water potential includes signed comparisons and a worked zero-change calibration intercept.
- Countercurrent exchange compares adjacent concentrations in opposite and parallel flows using explicitly illustrative concentrations.
- Calvin cycle stages reveal fixation, GP reduction, ATP and reduced NADP requirements, regeneration, and net TP output.
- Dihybrid inheritance models gametes and a complete 4 × 4 cross. Ratio predictions state the assumptions about segregation, dominance, interaction, and survival.
- Hardy–Weinberg distinguishes allele and genotype frequencies, with different worked, guided, and independent values.
- Gene regulation distinguishes promoter methylation, histone acetylation, activators, and repressors. Predictions state their conditions.

Catch-up pages for these topics and the cardiac cycle include the teaching activities. Existing figures and simulation links remain available alongside them. Students can collapse the teaching or skip directly to the established questions.

## Exam Skills

The new area is organised into biological drawings, understanding mark schemes, explaining mechanisms, unfamiliar contexts and causal reasoning, and scientific data/calculations/representations. Paper pages, topic pages, and Maths Skills link to it. Existing unfamiliar-data evaluation remains linked from the new area.

### Drawing practice

The bank contains 56 original schematic examples across leaf, dicot stem, young dicot root, artery, onion epidermis, cheek epithelium, and budding yeast. Examples use low-power tissue plans or cell drawings as stated in each task. Eight controlled variants per specimen include an acceptable drawing, each of the six defect categories, and a combination of line-quality and shading defects.

An introductory comparison models judgement before short, rotating six-drawing sets. Students select Accept/Reject and, for rejected drawings, the affected categories. Feedback identifies the category-specific defect. The reference observation, scale and task requirements stay beside the candidate. The schematic reference is an authored observation for comparison, rather than a photomicrograph. Drawing descriptions provide an alternative representation, although visual judgement still needs specialist user evaluation.

### Distinct marking points

Activities count categories, group statements, select replacements for repeated ideas, compare stronger responses, and review written improvements. Drawing examples explicitly treat continuous lines, no sketching, no overlaps, and no hanging lines as one category in the supplied framework. Other tasks use structure/function, practical methods, numerical comparisons, trends, and uncertainty. Every framework is question-specific.

### Cardiac pressures

Activities compare atrial, ventricular and aortic pressures, complete a causal chain, identify missing reasoning, interpret a schematic pressure crossover, and construct an explanation against a checklist. Pressure rise during early systole is distinguished from the later fall in volume during ejection.

### Causal reasoning

Five levels progress from direct inhibition through substrate/product consequences, double inhibition, unfamiliar neuronal and transcriptional pathways, to competing influences. They include reversible/irreversible inhibition, substrate exhaustion, membrane potential and threshold, and a hypothetical hormonal feedback pathway. The pathway diagram uses both arrowheads and blunt ends with a legend. Written explanations are self-reviewed against explicit scientific criteria.

## Hints, feedback and progress

New activities offer immediate graduated support. Guided answers, hinted answers, corrected answers, independently correct answers, and self-reviewed writing have separate local outcomes. Written prose receives a checklist without an automated mark. Drafts, responses, support use and correction history persist locally. Drawing sets can resume after navigation or reload. The existing reset control also clears the new activity progress.

The established lesson engine continues its hint and reteach follow-ups. A hinted first answer now contributes to completion but does not count towards the independently correct percentage. New independent-pass evidence is saved separately from historic best scores. Historic best scores remain recorded, and existing prerequisite-pass behaviour is retained. Historical scores cannot retrospectively establish whether help was used. An independent pass uses the existing 80% threshold and describes performance in this practice session.

## Verification

Local checks cover all 52 authored tasks and all 56 drawing variants, numeric input, grouping, multi-selection, writing comparison, saved support/correction states, and responsive accessibility at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 pixels. The existing site, flashcard, Human Biology and full Chromium smoke checks also passed during implementation. The new checks run in the existing accessibility workflow, alongside the established Chromium, Firefox, WebKit, and performance checks.

Automated accessibility checks inspect semantic names, form labels and contrast. Physical mobile devices, assistive software such as NVDA/VoiceOver, and pupil usability sessions remain untested. There is no claim of completed specialist screen-reader or classroom validation. The drawing bank is schematic and covers a limited specimen range. The 52 reasoning tasks are finite authored examples and can be extended through the shared family format.
