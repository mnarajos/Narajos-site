# Year 10 and 11 Science Catch-up content plan

The order below follows the school’s Year 10 and Year 11 science catalogues. C4 and B5 appear in both years. Use shared lessons with one progress key and add distinct subtopics to the appropriate catalogue. The AQA GCSE Combined Science Trilogy 8464 specification determines coverage and tier, and the school catalogues determine when topics are taught. Every lesson follows the existing catch-up journey from the opening visual through modelled steps, a check and an unfamiliar application. Link to the matching topic in the GCSE companion on completion.

| Year and topic | Scope, with Higher Tier flagged where relevant | Prerequisites, vocabulary and likely misconception | Big Picture Question, visual and progression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 · B3 Infection and response | 4.3 Communicable disease, examples, barriers, immunity, vaccination, medicines and development | Pathogen, antigen, antibody, vaccination, antibiotic and clinical trial. Bacteria and viruses differ. An antibiotic is not a viral treatment. | Why can a vaccine protect you before you meet the pathogen? Use exposure timelines. Compare barriers with specific defences, then apply to a different exposure. Add lessons on transmission, medicines and the order of drug testing. |
| 10 · B4 Bioenergetics | 4.4 Photosynthesis, limiting factors and required practical, respiration, exercise, metabolism. Interacting limiting factors and inverse-square relationship where prescribed for Higher Tier. | Chloroplast, glucose, respiration, anaerobic, limiting factor, oxygen debt. Plants respire. A plateau can show a constant positive rate. | Why does adding more light sometimes stop helping? Compare rate curves and practical readings. Transfer to a carbon dioxide change. Follow with respiration equations and exercise. |
| 10 · P3 Particle model | 6.3 Density practical, changes of state, internal energy, specific heat capacity and latent heat, particle motion in gases. | Density, internal energy, latent heat, particle, pressure. Particles keep their size. A melting plateau does not mean the energy transfer stopped. | Where does the energy go while ice stays at 0 °C? Match a heating curve to the existing particle model. Then use an unfamiliar plateau and separately compare equal-volume blocks and constant-volume gas heating. |
| 10 · P4 Radioactivity | 6.4 Atomic models, isotopes, radiation types, decay equations, activity, half-life and contamination. Higher Tier includes numerical ratio of activity reduction. | Nucleus, isotope, ionising radiation, activity, half-life, irradiation, contamination. Individual decays are unpredictable. Irradiating an object need not make it radioactive. | Can anyone predict when this nucleus will decay? Reveal population counts at successive half-lives. Apply to reading a decay curve. Add atomic models, shielding and risk cases. |
| 10 · P2 Electricity | 6.2 Current, potential difference, resistance, circuit symbols, I–V and resistance practicals, series/parallel, domestic electricity, power and energy, National Grid. | Charge, current, potential difference, resistance, power. Current is not used up. Compare branch current with series current. | Why can each light in a home work on its own? Trace junctions and compare two circuits. Apply to a faulty branch. Follow with I–V graphs, domestic safety and energy transfers. |
| 10 · C3 Quantitative chemistry | 5.3 Relative formula mass, conservation, mass changes and concentration. Higher Tier includes moles, equations and limiting reactants. | Relative formula mass, conservation, mole, limiting reactant, concentration. Mass reading can fall when gas leaves; coefficients compare amounts in moles. | How can a fizzing flask seem to lose mass? Compare a sealed and open system. Transfer from formula mass to a mole calculation only in the Higher Tier extension. |
| 10/11 · C4 Chemical changes | 5.4 Reactivity, extraction, acids and salts practical, electrolysis. Higher Tier covers electron transfer, half equations and strong/weak acids. | Reactivity, oxidation, reduction, pH, neutralisation, electrode, electrolyte. Strength differs from concentration. Aqueous electrolysis can produce hydrogen instead of a reactive metal. | Why does molten salt give a different product from salt solution? Compare labelled ions and electrodes. Practise one molten and one aqueous case. Add lessons on metals, salts and acid strength. |
| 10 · C5 Energy changes | 5.5 Exothermic/endothermic reactions, reaction profiles, temperature-change practical. Bond-energy calculations for Higher Tier. | Activation energy, exothermic, endothermic, bond energy. Breaking bonds requires energy. | How can a reaction release heat when breaking bonds takes energy? Show a profile from the starting line and compare energy in and out. Transfer to an endothermic profile. |
| 10/11 · B5 Homeostasis | 4.5 Homeostasis, nervous system and reaction-time practical, endocrine control, blood glucose, reproductive hormones and contraception. Use separately flagged Higher Tier detail on glucagon feedback and fertility treatment and hormone interactions as specified. | Stimulus, receptor, effector, synapse, hormone, negative feedback. A hormone acts on target organs. Reflexes do not require a conscious decision. | How does your body notice and correct a change you did not feel? Step through blood glucose or a reflex arc. Transfer to a changed receptor, effector or regulatory condition. |
| 10 · B7 Ecology | 4.7 Communities, biotic and abiotic factors, adaptation, food chains, quadrats and transects practical, material cycles, biodiversity and impacts. | Population, community, ecosystem, competition, quadrat, biodiversity. A population differs from a community. Biomass, abundance and biodiversity are separate measures. | Why could one small environmental change affect many species? Trace one food-web change, then test a prediction using sample counts. Follow with cycles and biodiversity. |
| 11 · C6 Rates of reaction | 5.6 Rates, collision theory, catalysts, rate practical and reversible reactions. Tier flags on tangent and equilibrium effects as specified. | Rate, catalyst, activation energy, reversible, dynamic equilibrium. A steeper slope is a faster rate. At equilibrium both reactions still occur. | How can two reactions make the same amount of gas and finish at different times? Compare two graphs. Change one factor, then apply the same reasoning to a different curve. Add reversibility/equilibrium separately. |
| 11 · C7 Organic chemistry | 5.7 Crude oil, hydrocarbons, fractional distillation, trends, cracking and alkenes as covered by Trilogy. | Hydrocarbon, alkane, alkene, fraction, cracking. Distillation separates molecules; cracking changes them. | How can one barrel of crude oil become both fuel and chemical feedstock? Follow fractions in a tower, then compare a cracking model. |
| 11 · C8 Chemical analysis | 5.8 Pure substances, formulations, chromatography practical and gas tests. | Formulation, solvent front, stationary phase, mobile phase, Rf. Measure both chromatography distances from the baseline. A pure substance has one substance, not simply a natural appearance. | How can one black ink leave several colours on paper? Reveal the chromatogram and annotate distances. Apply to an unknown mixture. Add gas test observations. |
| 11 · C9 Chemistry of the atmosphere | 5.9 Early atmosphere, changes, greenhouse gases and climate, carbon footprint and pollutants. | Greenhouse gas, infrared radiation, carbon footprint, combustion. Greenhouse warming and ozone depletion are different. Carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide have different risks. | Why does it stay warmer on Earth than a bare-rock model predicts? Trace incoming and outgoing radiation, then connect a different example to climate or pollution. |
| 11 · C10 Using resources | 5.10 Resources, potable water and water practical, waste water, recycling and life-cycle assessment. Bioleaching and phytomining in the Higher Tier where prescribed. | Potable, filtration, desalination, life cycle, finite. Potable does not mean chemically pure. Life-cycle conclusions depend on their boundaries. | Can safe drinking water still contain dissolved substances? Compare water-treatment routes. Apply to rainwater or seawater and add a separate material life-cycle comparison. |
| 11 · P5 Forces | 6.5 Forces, extension practical, work, motion and graphs, laws of motion, acceleration practical, stopping, and momentum where prescribed for Higher Tier. | Resultant, acceleration, reaction time, elastic limit, momentum. Zero resultant means no acceleration. Thinking and braking distance differ. | Can a cyclist carry on moving while the forces balance? Compare agreed force arrows with the motion arrow. Transfer to a horizontal graph, then cover extension, acceleration and stopping. |
| 11 · P6 Waves | 6.6 Wave types, properties, wave speed required practical, electromagnetic spectrum, applications and hazards. Treat Higher Tier electromagnetic detail according to the specification. | Oscillation, wavelength, frequency, amplitude, compression, infrared. The pattern travels while particles oscillate locally. | If a wave travels along a rope, why does a marked piece of rope stay there? Step an oscillation and track one point. Transfer to a compression wave and spectrum contexts. |
| 11 · P7 Magnetism and electromagnets | 6.7 Poles, magnetic fields and electromagnets; motor effect, force equation and motor operation for Higher Tier. | Pole, induced magnetism, field, solenoid, motor effect. Field lines are a model. Current in the coil creates a field, and reversing it swaps the poles. | How can a magnet be turned off instantly? Compare the same coil with current off and on. Apply to an unfamiliar electromagnet and its field. |
| 11 · B6 Genetics | 4.6 Reproduction, inheritance, variation, evolution, selective breeding, genetic engineering, evidence and classification. Label Higher Tier detail where appropriate. | Gene, allele, genotype, phenotype, meiosis, selection. Dominant does not mean more common. Individuals do not evolve in response to a need. | How can two unaffected parents have an affected child? Model a Punnett square one gamete at a time, then test a new cross. Add genetic technologies, evolution evidence and classification in further lessons. |
