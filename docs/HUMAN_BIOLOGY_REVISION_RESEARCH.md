## What “high-yield” means here

This is a **new qualification**. The public materials presently support three different types of evidence, which must not be conflated:

1. **Mandatory/eligible specification content:** every relevant specification statement can be examined, even if it is absent from the sample paper. No subject should be excluded merely because it is not in the SAM.
2. **Demonstrated assessment *format*:** OCR's SAM and annotated guide establish that diagrams, calculations, data interpretation, objective questions, short explanations and longer analysis/evaluation are part of the intended approach.
3. **Illustrative sample-paper content:** the topics appearing in SAMs are good starting points for practice, but do not establish how frequently those same topics will be examined in future live series.

The app therefore uses **“sample-informed”**, not “most frequently examined”, and does not claim item frequency estimates. The current revised content bank is still only a pilot.

## Exam-level assessment design

Both F170 and F171 have 60-mark examinations lasting 75 minutes. OCR's F171 assessment guide describes knowledge (PO1), application (PO2), and analysis/evaluation (PO3), and notes that examination questions sample all five topic areas. F171 includes a 6-mark **analyse** and a 9-mark **discuss** question using levels of response. For these longer questions the app supplies a model and a checklist for *self-assessment*, not purported automatic essay marks or an official OCR mark scheme.

The default app journey is deliberately **choose examined unit → choose a mode/topic → answer → get precise feedback → revisit misconceptions**. The 'Learn' material is optional support rather than mandatory reading ahead of every question.

## Question design and acceptance criteria

Each question must have:
- One unambiguous, scientifically defensible expected answer, or an explicit self-assessment checklist for genuinely open responses.
- A clear specification unit and topic-area mapping, and a meaningful reason for selecting its format.
- An explanation of *why* the answer works, not just a repetition of the answer.
- A specific plausible misconception that explains why a distractor is wrong.
- For numeracy, correct values, unit conversions, acceptable precision, and input handling that avoids crediting malformed responses.
- For labelled diagrams, accessible descriptive text and an original, well-aligned illustration.
- Alternatives/synonyms where precise meaning supports them; never unsafe fuzzy acceptance of biologically distinct terms.
- For extended response, a reasonable model and criteria reflecting the demands of analyse/discuss, with no fictitious numeric automated award.

Quick Mix is a **confidence-first mixed set**: two accessible warm-ups (on-ramp), two core questions, followed by application. It diversifies topics and formats within each stage, but does not shuffle difficulty indiscriminately. After difficult early answers it can insert extra core practice, and only upgrades its final question to Stretch after strong independent performance. Quick checks must test the same specification subtopic at no greater difficulty; where no appropriate question exists, the missed or hint-assisted item returns later instead of falsely implying reteaching. Spaced flashcard history is kept separate from the AQA products.

## Current bank and remaining coverage gaps

As of this revision:
- F170: **28 learning sections** across four topic areas, **112 recall cards**, and **64 varied challenge questions** (including one accessible area warm-up per area).
- F171: **52 varied challenge questions** across all five topic areas, including one original 6-mark analyse and one 9-mark discuss example. This is a **preview**, not an exhaustive revision course.
- Formats: one-answer selection, select-two, ordering, matching, numerical calculation, short written recall, extended writing with self-check, original schematic/graph interpretation, and **choose the better explanation then justify**.
- Both previously uncovered F170 subsections, **1.2.1** (levels of organisation) and **2.1.1** (physiology versus anatomy), now have original varied-format questions. All 28 F170 section headings are represented, although coverage of their individual teaching statements has not been verified.
- F171 now has at least one question mapped to each of its **18 specification subtopic headings**, including 3.2.1 immune dysfunction, 3.2.2 vaccines, 1.3 communicable diseases and 5.1 reporting/analytics. These are often only one or a few items per section: data-rich interpretation, individual listed diseases, extended responses and comprehensive conceptual coverage still require substantial expansion.
- The whole bank still needs an independent, statement-by-statement specification audit and live browser/visual/accessibility testing. A passing syntax or smoke test is not a scientific peer review.

## Next expansion policy

Prioritise **untested specification coverage**, **transferable exam skills**, **representative data and clinical contexts**, then add variety within high-impact areas. Do not overfit to sample-paper topics: OCR explicitly samples across its topic areas. Keep F172–F177 out of assessed coursework assistance; teaching their underlying *biological knowledge* is appropriate, but there must be no generation, marking or coaching of live NEA assignments. Do not republish copyrighted OCR question stems, diagrams or mark schemes verbatim.

Avoid claiming any question is from a live OCR examination, is guaranteed to appear, or predicts future frequency.

### Confidence-first Quick Mix (21 November 2026)

After testing the app as a first-time student, the former Quick Mix was judged too abrupt because it shuffled questions across difficulty levels. The default now begins with two explicitly selected, GCSE-accessible recall questions, followed by two core-knowledge questions and then progressively harder application. Randomness varies topics and formats *within* these stages, never putting an application or advanced reproductive question first. After early difficulty, the question engine can substitute an additional core item before proceeding. A Stretch finale is only selected after at least six of the first seven base questions were correct independently; low early success instead leads to another core question. Responses are still checked deterministically or self-rated, and only local progress is used. Existing unattempted Quick Mix sessions are migrated to the new order; started sessions remain resumable.

This arrangement supports early successes without confusing a simple warm-up with demonstrated mastery. Avoid treating the first two correct answers as a secure skill claim. Difficult questions remain available in Exam-style focus and Challenge modes.

## Oracle pedagogy implementation (21 November 2026)

The authoritative shared learning-design standard is [`ORACLE_KEYSTONE_PEDAGOGY.md`](ORACLE_KEYSTONE_PEDAGOGY.md). Human Biology now distinguishes progressive **topic-area practice** (warm-up → core → application → challenge) from mixed revision (interleaved topic and format selection). The question bank adds four targeted error-correction questions and six two-step scientific reasoning questions. Optional graduated hints record **guided** success separately from unaided first-attempt recall. Wrong/unknown answers receive immediate explanation and up to two related quick checks per session, where sufficiently close existing questions are available. This is a transparent rule-based system, not AI grading. It is an interim step: concept-by-concept micro-lessons and independent verification of retention require more questions per section.

Only browser-local progress and session state are saved; written responses are discarded after moving to the next question. The Human Biology homepage provides a control to clear the saved local state. There is no answer-upload or analytics integration in this application. Hosting providers may still process technical connection information, described in the Privacy page.

## Visual learning and knowledge-only NEA unit bank (21 November 2026)

The separate **Other units** pathway now contains **78 original knowledge questions** across F172 Genetics, F173 Biomedical techniques, F174 Nutrition and metabolism, F175 Human reproduction, F176 The brain and F177 Drug development (13 each). Each includes an original schematic/graph interpretation task, and diagram questions show the same figure with short, annotated feedback after checking. These are early teaching-content examples, **not coverage-complete unit lessons** and not alternative coursework assignments. No NEA submission, assignment evidence or task-specific marking is accepted or produced.

Human Biology's F170/F171 question bank now has **116 examined-unit starter questions** (64 F170 and 52 F171). Alongside 78 knowledge-only challenges for F172–F177, this gives **194 challenges across all eight units** in ten formats, in addition to 112 F170 flashcards and the earlier explanatory prompts. The broader question-development rules are in [HUMAN_BIOLOGY_QUESTION_WRITING_STANDARD.md](HUMAN_BIOLOGY_QUESTION_WRITING_STANDARD.md).

The previous screen-reader labels on six diagrams disclosed the expected answer; these have been replaced with neutral descriptions. Diagrams and new unit banks require **manual scientific review and real browser accessibility/visual QA** before being considered ready for widespread pupil use. OCR's set assignments and rules for teacher feedback remain controlling: the app is for separate disciplinary knowledge, not assessed work.

## Companion roles (November 2026)

The Human Biology application now provides an optional [Catch up](/human-biology/#catchup) pathway. F170 recaps are built from the 28 separately authored F170 section notes; F171 now also has dedicated recaps for immune dysfunction (3.2.1) and vaccine types (3.2.2), while F171 and F172–F177 retain short general topic-area notes (not complete teaching courses). An uncertain/wrong response may link to the appropriate short concept or area explanation. This supports general subject understanding and independent revision, not any live NEA task. AQA A-level Biology also now offers a catch-up route from its homepage and concept list using its pre-existing concept key ideas; its existing question feedback, local progress and reteach system remain the primary learning workflow.
