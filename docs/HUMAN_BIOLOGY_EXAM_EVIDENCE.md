# Cambridge OCR AAQ Human Biology: exam evidence and resource decisions

Reviewed 21 November 2026 for H049/H149. This distinguishes published assessment design, sample-paper evidence and our teaching inferences.

## Sources and limits

The primary evidence reviewed is the [specification v8, October 2026](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/697578-specification-cambridge-advanced-national-in-human-biology.pdf), the [F170 sample QP and MS v3](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/697850-fundamentals-of-human-biology.pdf), the [F171 sample QP and MS v2](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/697856-health-and-disease.pdf), the [annotated F170 assessment guide](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/722892-fundamentals-of-human-biology-annotated-.pdf), the [annotated F171 guide](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/722893-health-and-disease-annotated-.pdf) and the [general assessment guide](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/711477-understanding-the-assessment-examined-and-moderated.pdf).

OCR directs centres to [Teach Cambridge for recent papers and mark schemes](https://support.ocr.org.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/33534183222546-Where-can-I-find-past-exam-papers-for-the-examined-units) and [recent examiner reports](https://support.ocr.org.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/16999740884370-Where-can-I-access-the-most-recent-past-papers-mark-schemes-and-examiner-reports). Neither a common-error frequency table nor predictions of frequently examined topics can be justified from the reviewed samples.

## What the exams demand

| Feature | F170 Fundamentals of human biology | F171 Health and disease |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Written examination, 60 marks, 75 minutes | Written examination, 60 marks, 75 minutes |
| Coverage | Four topic areas; at least one question or subpart from each | Five topic areas; at least one question or subpart from each |
| Knowledge/understanding (PO1) | 35–45 marks in the annotated guide | 5–15 marks in the annotated guide |
| Application (PO2) | Remaining 15–25 marks, calculated from the total | Remaining 30–40 marks, calculated after PO1 and the 15 PO3 marks |
| Analysis/evaluation (PO3) | Not assessed in this exam | One six-mark analyse and one nine-mark discuss question |
| Common item forms | Controlled choice, short answer, diagram/photomicrograph tasks, calculations, concise point-marked explanations | The same short forms plus the two levels-marked extended responses |

Both exams have mandatory questions, with no optional section. The specification requires mathematical skills in at least 10% of externally assessed marks. Subtopics are sampled over time; one sample cannot delimit the future content. The broader qualification also assesses practical/applied performance through NEA; PO4 should not be mistaken for an extra exam section.

## Breadth or depth?

The teaching conclusion is **broad coverage with precise understanding, followed by application and appropriate depth**. F170's emphasis on PO1 does not mean rote naming alone: the objective includes understanding, and its short questions can require reasons. F171 needs knowledge to be usable in clinical, practical and data contexts; the extended responses require connected and contextual analysis or discussion.

The specification's teaching-content and breadth/depth columns jointly define assessable scope. Unless an entry is explicitly knowledge-only, OCR expects understanding. The exclusions are equally important: for example, the organelle-link section excludes transcription/translation detail and respiratory chemistry, while microscopy excludes the underlying physics. Conversely F170 expressly includes named mitotic/meiosis phases, ribosome types and specific cell/organ structures. A minimal GCSE Combined Science treatment would omit required AAQ content; importing every A-level detail would add unnecessary load.

## Concrete sample-paper evidence

| Evidence | Detail and response demand | Resource decision |
|---|---|---|
| F170 Q1: cells, organelles and stem cells | Structure recognition and specific functions, not a general statement that an organelle is important | Practise names, functions, and recognising the same structure in another representation |
| F170 Q2: microscopy and mitotic images | A microscopy advantage must relate to the supplied image; phase identification is paired with visible evidence | Require an observation supporting an identification; generic advantages may not answer the task |
| F170 Q4: peak-flow procedure | Two improvements with linked reasons can account for four marks; the best valid effort is recorded | Use error correction and action–reason pairs, not just memorised procedure lists |
| F170 Q5: glucose feedback and calculations | Explain a response using the supplied feedback model; calculations require correct reference values and units | Teach the causal loop and interpretation of a new diagram; show working and conversion steps |
| F171 Q3: antibiotic test | Select from the evidence, justify the choice and explain the control | Pair a conclusion with its evidential reason; distinguish an experimental result from a complete clinical decision |
| F171 Q4 and Q6: antibody diagram and workflow | Specific structural labels and a multi-step ordering task | Include original diagrams, matching and sequencing, with partial understanding made visible in feedback |
| F171 Q5: monitoring options | Either offered option can earn credit if the reasons fit it | Some justified choices need open self-assessment rather than a single supposedly universal preferred option |
| F171 Q7 and Q12: clinical table and chart | Calculate a summary with requested precision; extract observations from supplied data | Vary values and representations; distinguish observation, calculation and explanation |
| F171 Q13: actions in a consultation | An action needs a contextual reason, with two linked points per explained action | Teach action → reason → relevance, avoiding generic responses pasted into every scenario |
| F171 Q14/Q15: extended responses | Analyse effects in the person's circumstances; discuss benefits/limitations and justify a recommendation | Model linked reasoning and contextual judgement; a list of isolated facts is insufficient |

The examples above describe the response demands; our app questions and scenarios are independently authored. The six- and nine-mark indicative content is not a checklist where every listed point must appear. Levels reward the quality, scope, precision and contextual use of reasoning. The app must not invent an automated examination score for a free explanation.

## How this changes resource building

1. Map a concept to the actual breadth/depth statement, not only its subtopic heading. Record required terminology, links and exclusions.
2. Build short sequences from accessible retrieval through explanation and discrimination to contextual use. A hard context should supply unfamiliar facts needed to answer.
3. Write a family of alternatives for each assessed objective. Change representation, wording, quantities, errors or context while preserving the idea and broad demand. Select one per ordinary set; use an independent sibling after difficulty where suitable.
4. Use first-principles support: a strategy, then the relevant biological relationship, then optional limited narrowing. Reveal the answer and a worked explanation separately. Do not make a hint an answer with one word removed by default.
5. Keep help visually quiet. Show Hint after ten seconds without an answer interaction; typing, choosing and ordering restart the delay. A keyboard user must be able to activate a visible hint.
6. Treat successful answers after help as guided. Revisit independently before implying security; short practice scores do not prove retention.
7. Expand complete statement coverage as well as variants. A large number of alternatives for one idea cannot substitute for missing content elsewhere.

## Implemented in this release

170 new original alternatives join 85 three-version question families. Every F170/F171 subtopic heading now has at least one such family. The fixed bank is 364 questions: 156 F170, 130 F171 and 78 knowledge-only F172–F177 questions. The count includes alternatives, not 364 distinct learning objectives. The separate 14 generated skill families remain available.

Normal new sets select one variant of a family, and recent local selection history discourages immediate repeats. Unfinished sets restore their original version. Wrong or guided answers prefer a same-objective check before another question merely sharing the subtopic. Families have authored two-step prompts/principles; eligible four-choice questions can then offer limited narrowing. Full statement-by-statement coverage and additional clinical/graph contexts remain ongoing content work.
