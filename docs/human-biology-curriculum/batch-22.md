# F171 communicable diseases, transmission and evidence

Batch 21 was merged at 39b6dff. The site and browser workflows passed on main and no pull requests were open. The audit used the October 2026 OCR Version 8 specification, pp. 51–52, the F171 register, catch-up content, recall cards, practice bank, rotating families and progressions. OCR specifies vaginal thrush for candidiasis. A route, a portal of entry and a causative organism answer three different questions.

| Named disease | Prior teaching, application and feedback | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| COVID-19 | Pandemic context does not teach how to interpret the findings in a symptomatic case. | Compare a respiratory virus with HIV. |
| HIV/AIDS | Immune dysfunction mentions HIV. It does not distinguish infection from advanced disease in an evidence task. | Reuse immune dysfunction; teach the distinction and limits of symptoms. |
| Lyme disease | Bacterial-group entry was supported by TB evidence only. | Teach vector, prevention and rash evidence. |
| MRSA | Infection-control practice does not assess colonisation versus wound infection. | Compare evidence from a screen with findings in a wound. |
| Tuberculosis | Existing TB family and progression teach bacterial cause, airborne pulmonary transmission and prevention with feedback. | Keep these; address symptom inference. |
| Candidiasis | F170 names Candida as a fungus, with no application to vaginal symptoms. | Teach overgrowth and the limits of symptoms. |
| Histoplasmosis | No mapped disease-specific worked or independent case. | Contrast environmental exposure with endogenous overgrowth. |
| Malaria | Existing red-cell family explains anaemia and fatigue with diagnostic feedback. | Retain; add vector comparison and interpretation of blood-film evidence. |
| Toxoplasmosis | No independent task mapped to this named protozoan. | Contrast food and congenital routes with mosquito transmission. |
| Fasciolosis | No named evidence task mapped. | Compare food exposure and possible hepatobiliary findings. |
| Hydatid disease | Farming context alone did not teach how infection is acquired or how to interpret cyst evidence. | Distinguish route from occupation and scan evidence from symptoms. |

Source: [OCR Human Biology specification](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/697578-specification-cambridge-advanced-national-in-human-biology.pdf#page=51).
