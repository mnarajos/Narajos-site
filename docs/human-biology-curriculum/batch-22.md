## Delivered teaching and assessment

Five searchable Area 1 lessons cover all eleven examples. Each includes a comparison table, worked reasoning, a guided check and an independent case. The five cases require three constructed explanations (viral, fungal and protozoan comparisons) and two objective decisions (bacterial and helminth cases). Two additional practice-bank items check classification and prevention in new contexts. The TB and malaria routes lead on to their established progressions. The existing course-wide method, immunity and treatment routes keep their original content and identifiers.

The MRSA case contrasts an intact-skin screen with an untested painful wound. The blood-film case contrasts an identified organism in one patient with nonspecific examination findings in another. The two original diagrams have text equivalents. No laboratory identification proficiency is inferred from interpreting a supplied report. Written explanations use the established first-draft, criteria, model and self-review workflow.

All 21 affected rows now name the relevant lesson and assessment evidence in [the F171 register](f171.md). Four moved to Verified: selecting prevention by route, interpreting MRSA records, weighing malaria evidence and recognising limits of toxoplasmosis inference. Within these two headings the remaining seventeen are Taught. Across F171, Verified rose from 28 to 32 and Taught from 21 to 38. These are reviewed resource classifications. They do not measure learners’ attainment.

The most useful remaining practice would require a pupil to distinguish features of TB, Lyme disease and histoplasmosis in fresh cases, interpret an unfamiliar microscopic representation without a supplied identification, and distinguish portals of entry without a prompt. Separate recall of the parent-to-child and perinatal examples is not evidence of independent application. No observed pupil trial was available.
