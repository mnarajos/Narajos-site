# Plan an investigation: scientific and assessment review

This record supports review of the AQA A-level Biology investigation-planning sequence. The four briefs are original teaching tasks; they are not AQA examination questions or mark schemes. The activities teach planning, not laboratory competence. A correct structured choice or a completed revision is not evidence that an unrestricted written method would receive examination credit.

## Calibration

[AQA practical assessment](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/practical-assessment) includes practical problem solving and experimental design, and identifies investigations of solute concentration and membrane permeability among the required practicals. [AQA's assessment scheme](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/biology/a-level/biology-7402/specification/scheme-of-assessment) includes developing and refining procedures in AO3. Written assessment of practical skills and the practical endorsement are distinct.

The [June 2024 Paper 2 mark scheme, question 08.4](https://www.aqa.org.uk/files/sample-papers-and-mark-schemes.2024.June.AQA-74022-MS-JUN24_PDF), accepts several measurements and allocations in its particular growth-regulator question. It is useful evidence that a single expression or allocation should not be imposed on a different task. Credit in a particular examination question depends on its wording and mark scheme; a valid improvement to a design is not necessarily a required mark point. The seedling task uses a different original brief and individually treated pots.

## Progression

The first three tasks retain planning areas so pupils can inspect a whole plan while working on a manageable part. Structured choices demonstrate planning with that support; they are not evidence of an unaided examination response. The checker assesses represented decisions and authored relationships, not unrestricted written methods or explanations. Help that supplies planning decisions is recorded separately from incidental vocabulary and accessibility support.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

This is guided construction, not unaided planning. |
| Beetroot | Apparatus and colorimetry are explained; a partial proposal uses 10 cm³ at 20 and 40 °C but 20 cm³ at 60 °C. | Diagnose the volume confound and repair it by retaining 20 and 40 °C at 10 cm³ or choosing a common volume across all three temperatures. Supply allocation and duration and describe the procedure. | Worked help is available on request. The checker evaluates the represented diagnosis, repair and relationships; free writing remains unassessed. |

This is more integrated planning with prompts, not an unstructured examination answer. |
| Cress | The first attempt can be made from the brief without planning headings or a checklist. Follow-up choices and either review route do not certify either written response. |

## Scientific decisions to review

| Context | Intended inference and comparison | Key design relationships and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potato and sucrose | Compare percentage mass changes of potato pieces after exposure to prepared sucrose concentrations. | Measure initial and final mass for each piece, with consistent exposure and blotting. Calculate each piece's change as a percentage of its own nonzero initial mass before averaging by treatment. The supplied cylinder measures to 0.5 cm³; values from 10 to 30 cm³ in 0.5 cm³ increments are allowed. The task does not require dilution design or a water-potential estimate. |
| Beetroot and temperature | Compare pigment released into solution after equal periods at different temperatures. | Standardise tissue preparation, solution volume and time; rinse consistently. Changing solution volume with temperature can affect concentration and therefore absorbance. The cylinder measures to 0.5 cm³; the allowed 10–30 cm³ range includes 0.5 cm³ increments. The water blank sets the colorimeter; it is not an experimental treatment. |
| Growth regulator and seedling stems | The question is: “Does the concentration of a supplied growth regulator affect the increase in stem length of young seedlings over seven days?” Comparing the supplied 1 and 5 mg dm⁻³ treatments addresses differences across those tested concentrations; a matching-solvent zero treatment supports an additional comparison with no added regulator. | Initial and final lengths support each seedling's increase. If each starting length is known to be 40 mm, subtracting that known value from each final length also gives each increase. A randomised comparison of final outcomes can estimate a treatment contrast, but does not directly provide each seedling's growth. Percentage increase is relative to starting length and can yield a different pattern from absolute increase. Change alone cannot remove confounding. |
| Cress and salt | Compare germination after five days in salt and zero-salt conditions under the supplied conditions. | Compare counts or proportions of seeds germinated; proportions permit comparison of unequal groups. Root length alone addresses growth, not the stated germination outcome. Keep light uniform or balance treatments across positions. Conclusions apply to these seeds and conditions. |

Allocation numbers count specimens. One specimen in each treatment vessel is individually treated; multiple specimens in one shared vessel do not constitute multiple independent applications of that treatment. Repeated instrument readings do not create new specimens. Repetition or averaging cannot rescue an otherwise confounded comparison. Controls must be appropriate to the stated investigation; they are not interchangeable labels.

## Review protocol

Use the actual activity and its tests to record evidence against these cases before release:

- A pupil needing help can use the worked potato decision, see the whole plan and repair a specific problem. The beetroot task retains a familiar representation and available worked help without presupposing colorimetry.
- Each first-three plan connects a treatment comparison to measurements, timing, allocation, conditions and processing. Individually reasonable choices that conflict receive actionable feedback at the decision that needs changing.
- Feasible alternative allocations and defensible measurements are not rejected solely for differing from an example. Missing details and approaches outside authored checks are not described as scientifically wrong.
- An edited decision invalidates dependent success feedback. Rechecking evaluates the current plan; a corrected structured response is not described as an independently generated examination answer.
- In the transfer activity, an uncued initial response is stored before checks appear, while pupils who need a start may request planning support before writing. The initial response, follow-up decisions and review or revision remain distinguishable after reload. Follow-up correctness does not mark the original response. A pupil who considers the original complete can record that review without making an arbitrary edit.
- Word definitions and accessibility support remain available without implying that planning decisions were supplied. Learner-controlled help can be opened on a narrow screen and does not obstruct revision.
- Verify keyboard and touch flows, feedback announcement and focus, zoom and narrow layouts, local save/resume/reset and unavailable storage. Confirm that older A-level progress keys and identifiers remain intact.

For each release, attach the exact commit, test commands and outcomes, captures of desktop and phone journeys, and any unresolved accessibility or scientific-review findings. Automated checks and screenshots do not establish pupil attainment or replace a teacher's review of written work. For learning evidence, compare independently written responses to different unseen tasks before and after practice, recording support and revisions separately and accepting other defensible approaches.
