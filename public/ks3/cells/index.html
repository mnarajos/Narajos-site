Cells and microscopes

How can you recognise a plant cell?

Start here. Find the leaf cell’s wall. Then find the thin cell membrane in both diagrams.

These are teaching diagrams. Colours and sizes are simplified.

Structures shared by these two cells

  • The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
  • The cytoplasm is the jelly-like material where many cell reactions happen.
  • The nucleus contains DNA, which carries genetic information.
  • Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration.
  • Ribosomes make proteins. The diagrams show them as tiny dots.

Structures in the green leaf cell

  • The cell wall is a rigid layer outside the cell membrane. It supports the cell.
  • Chloroplasts absorb light for photosynthesis. Some plant cells, such as onion bulb cells, usually lack chloroplasts.
  • The large vacuole contains cell sap and helps keep the cell firm.

Check what you spotted

Sample A is plant tissue. This photo shows cells in a pondweed (Elodea) leaf. The lines around neighbouring cells show the cell walls. The small green bodies inside the cells are chloroplasts.

Sample B is animal tissue. These are stained human cheek cells. You can see thin cell outlines and dark pink nuclei. Nuclei and cell membranes are features shared by plant and animal cells.

Use the structures you can identify as evidence. The chloroplasts inside these cells give evidence that Sample A is plant tissue. Animal cells can also have straight-looking edges.
