Particles and gas pressure

Why does gas pressure change?

Picture air inside a sealed box. The air particles are always moving. Each time a particle hits a wall, it pushes on it. Pressure is the force on each unit of area.

Try it. Start the motion and watch a few wall collisions. Predict what will happen to the pressure when you warm the gas, then switch to Warmer gas. Next, try Smaller box at the starting temperature.

Choose one change

Starting pressure. The particles bounce off the walls.

Read the explanation
  • Warm the gas in the same box. Particles move faster on average. They hit the walls more often and push harder overall, so the pressure rises.
  • Squeeze the gas at the same temperature. Average particle speed stays the same. The same particles have less space and hit the walls more often, so the pressure rises.

Try it yourself

The gas in a sealed, rigid container cools down. Explain why its pressure falls.

Compare your explanation

The particles move more slowly on average. They hit the walls less often and push less hard overall. The force on each unit of area falls, so the pressure goes down.
