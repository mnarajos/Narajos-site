The science behind the model

Valves follow pressure. A valve opens when the pressure behind it is greater than the pressure in front, and closes when the pressure in front is greater. So each crossing of two pressure lines on the graph is a valve opening or closing.

Heart sounds. The first sound (“lub”, S1) is the atrioventricular valves closing at the start of ventricular systole. The second (“dub”, S2) is the semilunar valves closing at the start of diastole.

Isovolumetric phases. Just after the AV valves close and just after the SL valves close, all four valves are shut. Pressure changes sharply but volume stays constant because no blood can enter or leave.

Filling is mostly passive. About 80% of ventricular filling happens passively before the atria contract; atrial systole tops up the rest (the “atrial kick”).

The SAN is the pacemaker. It sets the rhythm and sends a wave of depolarisation across both atria, making them contract. Atrial depolarisation produces the P wave on the ECG.

The AVN delays the impulse by about 0.1 s so the atria finish emptying before the ventricles contract. Non-conducting tissue between the atria and ventricles stops the impulse spreading directly, so the AVN is the only route.

His → Purkinje. The impulse travels down the bundle of His through the septum, then the Purkinje fibres carry it up through the ventricle walls, so the ventricles contract from the apex upwards. Ventricular depolarisation produces the QRS complex; the T wave represents ventricular repolarisation.

Activation precedes contraction. Electrical activation triggers the subsequent contraction of cardiac muscle.
