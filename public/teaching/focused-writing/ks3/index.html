    Practise because, but, and so

    Because gives the reason. “The dye spread further in warm water because …”

    But shows a contrast. “Both beakers had the same amount of dye, but …”

    So tells the reader what followed. “The dye particles moved faster in warm water, so …”

    Worked example question
    Why did a drop of blue dye spread further through warm water than cool water?

    Topic sentence
    Blue dye spreads faster through warmer water.
    Supporting notes
    • Same time in both beakers
    • Warmer water → faster random movement of dye particles
    • Dye spreads further from where it started
    Concluding sentence
    Faster random movement explains why we see more spreading in the warm water.
    Read the full paragraph

    Blue dye spreads faster through warmer water. The two drops have the same time to spread. In warmer water the dye particles move faster on average. They move randomly, so overall more dye spreads away from the place where it started. This explains why the blue patch is wider in the warm beaker.

    Plan your paragraph

    Write the main idea in one sentence. Jot down what happens first and next. Finish by linking your ideas back to the question.

    Use a short note for each step in the explanation. Say how the movement changes, then link it back to what you saw.
