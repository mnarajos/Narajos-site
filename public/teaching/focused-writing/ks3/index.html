KS3 · Explain a change

Start here. Point to the beaker where the colour has spread further. Say what you see: “The colour has spread further in the … water.”

Now explain. Why did the colour spread further in the same time?

See a worked example

Example question: Why does heating air in a closed, hard-sided bottle increase the pressure inside?

Topic sentence. Air pressure rises when a closed, hard-sided bottle is warmed.

Notes. same space → faster particles → more hits on walls → greater push on walls

Closing link. The walls feel a greater push per unit area. That means higher pressure.

Air pressure rises when a closed, hard-sided bottle is warmed. The bottle stays the same size and no air escapes. When the air gets warmer, its particles move faster on average. They hit the walls more often. The faster hits also have a greater effect on the walls. This increases the force on each square centimetre of wall, so the pressure rises.

Plan your paragraph

Write the main idea in one sentence. Jot down what happens first and next. Finish by linking your ideas back to the question.

Topic sentence to start with: Food colouring spreads faster through warm water.

Help ordering your notes

Warmer water → dye particles move faster on average → colour spreads faster. Try the starter “When the water is warmer, the dye particles …”

Compare your explanation

First check your main idea: the colour spreads further in warm water in the same time. Explain that the dye particles move randomly and move faster on average in warm water. Overall, more dye moves away from where it is crowded than moves back towards it. This spreading from a higher to a lower concentration is called diffusion.
