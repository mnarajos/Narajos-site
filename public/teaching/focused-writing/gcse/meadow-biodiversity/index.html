Evaluate a claim about biodiversity 

Biology Paper 2

Stretch: optional, especially challenging practice.

Plant sampling in two areas of the school field
AreaQuadrats sampledDifferent plant species found in all samples
Mown weekly64
Mown once a term69

Pupils used quadrats of the same size on one afternoon. There was only one area with each mowing routine.

Start here. Which area had more different plant species? Were both areas sampled the same number of times?

Your job. Which mowing routine would you recommend trialling on the school field to see whether it helps more plant species grow?

Now write. Evaluate the claim that mowing less often increases plant biodiversity. Use the results, explain why there might be a difference and suggest how to make the comparison more convincing.

      Compare with the key ideas
      • Did you compare 9 species with 4 and link that difference to the mowing routines?
      • Did you explain a possible cause? More time between cuts may let some plants flower, set seed, and reproduce.
      • Did you explain a limitation? The areas might also differ in shade, soil, or trampling, and one area per routine makes it hard to isolate mowing.
      • Did you give a cautious conclusion and suggest a way to improve the comparison, such as testing several similar areas for each mowing routine and placing quadrats randomly?
      Read a model paragraph

      The samples suggest that mowing less often may increase the number of plant species. Six quadrats found nine species in the area mown once a term and four in the area mown weekly. A longer gap between cuts could give more plants time to flower and set seed. The areas may also differ in shade or soil, and there was only one area for each routine. I would compare several similar areas for each mowing routine before drawing a firmer conclusion.
