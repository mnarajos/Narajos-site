Justify a choice using life-cycle data

Chemistry Paper 2

Both types carry the same amount per trip. CO₂e means carbon dioxide equivalent, a measure of greenhouse gas emissions. The table covers production and use.

Start here. Which bag has higher production emissions? How many paper bags would you need for ten trips?

Your job. Which bag should the school buy for ten trips? Convince someone using the figures and explain when your choice could change.

Now write. Which bag has lower emissions for ten trips? Compare the data, justify your choice and give one reason why it might change in real life.

Plan your paragraph

Organise your notes in this order. total for each bag → compare the totals → condition that could change your choice.

Help

This is the first sentence of your paragraph. Say the main idea and link it to the question. Try: “The results show that …”

Help

Work out the emissions for ten paper bags and for making and washing one sturdy bag. Explain why the number of uses can change the result. Think about another life-cycle stage.

Help

State which bag you would choose for ten trips on this evidence, and when that choice might change.

Write your paragraph
Help

Turn your notes into complete sentences. Put your main idea first, then add your supporting sentences. Use links such as “because”, “so” or “however” where they show how ideas fit together. Finish with your concluding sentence. Write it all as one paragraph.

    Compare with the key ideas
    • Did you calculate 240 g CO₂e for ten paper bags and 200 g CO₂e for one sturdy bag with ten washes?
    • Did you explain why the sturdy bag has lower emissions for ten trips in this example, even though making one produces more emissions?
    • Did you explain how fewer reuses or more emissions per wash could change the choice?
    • Did you identify another life-cycle stage, such as transport or disposal, that would need evidence for a wider comparison?
    Read a model paragraph

    The sturdy bag produces less greenhouse gas for ten trips in this example. Ten paper bags produce 10 × 24 = 240 g CO₂e. One sturdy bag produces 180 g CO₂e to make and 10 × 2 = 20 g CO₂e from washing, for a total of 200 g CO₂e. The sturdy bag therefore saves 40 g CO₂e over ten trips on these figures. If the sturdy bag is used fewer times, the paper bags could produce fewer emissions overall.
