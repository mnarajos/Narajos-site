Teaching with a single-paragraph outline

Begin with the visual. Ask pupils to say what they can see in everyday words. Then model one sentence that states the main idea, brief notes that explain it, and a final sentence that links back to the question. Add the scientific terms as pupils develop the explanation. Use sentence ordering to practise the structure first. Then use Check my outline to compare a pupil’s own plan against the task prompts before they write the full paragraph.

Use the first KS3 or GCSE sentence as a short classroom activity. Give pupils time to rehearse it aloud, check it, and make one revision. Then offer the partly filled plan or the independent plan. The fresh example checks whether pupils can use the same move again.

For a classroom trial, notice who starts without an adult supplying the first words, what improves on the second attempt, and whether pupils want to continue. The computer checks specific ideas in the two pilots. Use the pupil’s copied draft to review the overall explanation.
