Investigating respiration in yeast

Two ways to measure respiration: a timed colour change with methylene blue, and a gas-volume change in a respirometer. For any set-up, work out what is directly measured, what changes produce the reading, and what the reading lets you conclude.

Set-up

Kept the same for every tube: 2 cm³ of the assigned preparation (glucose only for the no-yeast control), 2 cm³ dilute methylene blue, one 10-second shake, end point judged against the colour standard, 600 s observation period.

Results

These readings are simulated: the model's assumptions
  • Methylene blue: end-point times are set to match the trial results in the AQA practical handbook (means of 275 s at 20 °C, 128 s at 35 °C and 145 s at 45 °C). Temperatures between these values are interpolated; behaviour outside 20–45 °C is illustrative modelling. Each tube varies by up to about 7% to mimic real repeats.
  • The dye stays blue while dissolved oxygen from the single 10-second shake re-oxidises it; once the oxygen runs low, reduction wins and the blue fades. The end point is "no blue left: matches the colour standard". Tubes that have not reached it after 600 s are recorded as not reached.
  • Carbon dioxide made by the yeast does not change methylene blue. Boiled yeast and tubes with no yeast never reduce the dye.
  • Respirometer: rates of oxygen uptake match the RP9 lesson (6, 14, 24 and 9 mm in 5 minutes at 15, 25, 35 and 45 °C with a 0.5 mm capillary). The aerobic case assumes aerobic glucose respiration and approximately balancing volumes of oxygen uptake and carbon dioxide production. Under nitrogen it respires anaerobically only.
  • Distance moved = volume change ÷ πr². Gas that has not reached the bath temperature expands or contracts. Each dot in the gas and molecule views stands for a large number of molecules; nitrogen is not shown.
